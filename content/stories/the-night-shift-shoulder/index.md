*A short story from The Velvet Notes, the SinDept blog. The characters and events are fictional.*

The third patient I helped boost up the bed that Tuesday was a retired roofer named Walt, and he apologized the whole time.

"I'm heavier than I look," he said.

"Everybody says that," I told him, which was true, and then I went to the supply room and stood there for a second with one hand on my right shoulder like it might fall off if I let go.

Twelve hours in the ER does something specific to your upper back. It's not a dramatic injury. It's a hundred small lifts and reaches and leaning over a bed rail at a bad angle while somebody's monitor beeps. By the time I got home to Clintonville at 7:50 a.m., I couldn't turn my head left without my whole body coming along for the ride.

I stood in the kitchen in my navy scrubs and tried to reach the spot myself. You know the move. Arm over the shoulder, two fingers pressing, elbow pointing at the ceiling like a confused swan.

*This is what thirty-four looks like. Massaging your own back with a spatula.*

I didn't actually use the spatula. I thought about it.

---

Dani found me in the break room two nights later doing neck rolls next to the microwave.

"You look like a pigeon," she said.

"Thank you, Dani."

She'd been a nurse for eleven years and had opinions about everything, but she delivered them like weather reports. Not mean. Just information.

"Get a wand," she said. "A massager. Rechargeable, so there's no cord to trip over at three in the morning."

"I'm not paying for a massage therapist on what they pay us."

"I didn't say therapist. I said wand." She took a pretzel out of my bag without asking. "You lean back on the couch, you put it on your shoulder, you stop making that face."

"What face?"

She made the face. It was accurate.

I said I'd think about it, which was a lie, because I already knew I'd go home and look. I'd gotten better at that lately. At admitting what I wanted before I'd talked myself out of it. A few weeks earlier I'd ordered something for myself for the first time in a long time, and the world hadn't ended. My neighbor hadn't even blinked.

So I looked.

---

I ended up ordering a [cordless wand body massager](https://sindept.com/products/cordless-wand-body-massager-10-modes-4-speeds/) from SinDept. Black, not red. Red felt like it would be sitting on my coffee table announcing itself.

What sold me was boring. The description said it was made for shoulders, back, arms and legs. Broad, flexible silicone head. A textured grip, which mattered, because my hands are always a little dry and slippery from hand sanitizer. Ten patterns and four speeds, which seemed like a lot of choices for someone who just wanted her trapezius to stop yelling. USB rechargeable. And it shipped in plain outer packaging with no product details on the label.

That last part mattered more than I wanted it to.

When the box showed up, Mr. Okafor from 1B was getting his mail at the same time. He looked at it, looked at me.

"More printer ink?"

"I print a lot," I said.

"Must be a big project."

I went upstairs and laughed so hard I had to sit down on the floor of my hallway.

---

Here is the part nobody tells you: I was annoyed at it first.

I plugged it in next to the toaster the minute I opened the box, and then I scrolled back to the product page on my phone, where it said not to use it while charging. I read that line twice, hoping it would say something different. It didn't. I stared at it like a kid waiting for cookies. Then I gave up and went to bed.

The next night, I sat on the couch with a heating pad behind me and put the wand on my shoulder at the lowest speed. It felt like someone tapping me politely to ask a question. I went up one. Then two. At four, it felt like I'd leaned against a running washing machine and my teeth agreed. I went back down to two and left it there, and then I made a noise I'm glad Mr. Okafor didn't hear.

It was just my shoulder. I want to be clear about that. For about twenty minutes it was just my shoulder, and the knot I'd been carrying since Walt the roofer finally started to give.

Then the shoulder was fine, and I was warm, and the apartment was very quiet, and the wand was still on.

I didn't plan it. I think that's why it worked. There was no decision to make and nobody to explain anything to. Just the heavy, slow feeling of a body that had been holding itself together for twelve hours, letting go one piece at a time. My breathing got loud enough that I noticed it. I laughed once, at nothing, at myself, at the fact that I'd bought this thing for my back. Then I stopped laughing.

Afterward I lay there with the throw blanket half on the floor and my heart going like I'd taken the stairs at work.

*Well,* I thought. *That's two things it's good for.*

---

I'm not going to tell you a massager changed my life. It didn't. I still work nights. My shoulder still hurts after a bad shift. Kyle still has the good skillet.

But I take ten minutes now when I get home. Couch, heating pad, speed two. I don't feel guilty about it. That's new.

Last week Dani asked if I'd ever gotten the wand.

"Yeah," I said.

"And?"

"And my shoulder's better."

She looked at me for a long second, the way she looks at a patient who says their pain is a three.

"Uh-huh," she said, and stole another pretzel.
