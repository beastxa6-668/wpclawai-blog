*A short story from The Velvet Notes, the SinDept blog. The characters and events are fictional.*

The baby monitor made a sound like a tiny dinosaur, and Dev and I both froze over the dishwasher like burglars.

It was 9:24 on a Thursday. Anika had gone down at 8:40, which in our house counts as a miracle. We'd been married eight years. She'd been alive for one of them. In that one year we had become two people who talked mostly in logistics. *Did you buy wipes. Is the daycare form signed. Whose turn is it.*

The dinosaur noise stopped. We both breathed out.

A few weeks earlier, in this same kitchen, at almost this same time, I'd been the one to bring something up. I'd been terrified. I'd asked if he'd ever want to try something new, and I'd made it very clear it was a question, not a plan. He'd said yes, he'd want to talk about it, and we had, a little, in the slow way you talk about anything when you're both running on four hours of sleep.

So I wasn't expecting it when Dev, rinsing a sippy cup, said, "Can I bring something up now?"

"Is it the daycare form?"

"It's not the daycare form."

He put the sippy cup in the rack very carefully. Dev is an accountant. He does everything very carefully. He proposed to me with a printed itinerary.

"I've been reading," he said. "Since we talked. And there's a thing I'm curious about. For me."

*Oh,* I thought. *Oh, it's his turn.*

---

It's strange how fast you can go from being the nervous one to the one who has to make it okay.

He told me what he'd been reading about. He talked to the dishwasher the whole time, not to me. His ears went red. He said "I don't know, it's probably dumb" twice, and both times I said "It's not dumb," and both times he nodded like he didn't believe me.

What I felt, honestly, was relief. Not because of the thing itself. Because for months I'd been worried I was the only one in this marriage who still wanted anything that wasn't sleep.

*He's been thinking about us too. In the middle of all the wipes and forms, he's been thinking about us.*

"Okay," I said. "Show me."

---

We looked at it together on his phone, sitting on the kitchen floor with our backs against the cabinets, because the couch was covered in laundry.

He'd already picked one: the [Vibrating Prostate Massager with Perineum Stimulator](https://sindept.com/products/vibrating-prostate-massager-perineum-stimulator/) on SinDept. He'd read the description enough times that he could recite it, which was extremely Dev.

"ABS and silicone," he said. "High-frequency vibration. About five inches long. Curved. Says it's for solo use or couples."

"Couples," I said.

"I wanted it to be something we did together," he said, and then he looked at me for the first time. "If you want. You can say no."

I scrolled down past the photos. Mostly the product by itself, plus one photo of a smiling couple in bed with the covers pulled up. Nothing that would make me want to close the tab. It said to start on a lower setting, to use water-based lube only, and to clean it with mild soap before and after. Plain outer packaging, no product details on the label.

"Thirty-four ninety-nine," I said.

"Less than the diaper subscription."

"Everything's less than the diaper subscription."

He laughed, and I leaned my head on his shoulder, and I said yes.

---

It came while Dev was at work. Our neighbor Mrs. Castellano had taken it in for us, the way she does. She brought it over with a tray of pizzelle.

"For you," she said, handing me both. "The cookies are from me. That's from Amazon, I think."

It wasn't from Amazon. I didn't correct her.

---

The first time didn't happen the first night. Anika had a fever. The second night we were too tired. The third night we sat on the edge of the bed and looked at the box and Dev said, "Should we just watch The Great British Baking Show," and I seriously considered it.

But we didn't.

I'm not going to tell you everything. I'll tell you that it was awkward for longer than anyone writes about. That we kept stopping to ask "Is this okay?" and "Lower?" and "Wait, which button?" That at one point the baby monitor made the dinosaur noise again and we both froze and stared at it for twenty full seconds, and it went quiet, and Dev started laughing so hard he had to put his face in the pillow.

And then, somewhere after that, it stopped being awkward.

I watched my careful husband stop being careful. I watched him close his eyes. His breathing got ragged and his hand found mine and held on hard. I'd never seen him like that, not in eight years, and I don't think he'd ever let himself be seen like that. There was a long, warm, shaky stretch where I didn't think about daycare forms at all.

Afterward he lay there with one arm over his face.

"You okay?" I asked.

"I'm going to need a minute," he said, muffled. Then, "Thank you."

"For what?"

He moved his arm and looked at me. "For not thinking it was dumb."

---

In the morning we were back to logistics. Wipes, forms, whose turn it was. Anika threw a whole banana on the floor.

But when Dev left for work, he kissed me at the door like he meant something by it. I stood in the kitchen with a banana on my sock and I smiled for no reason at all.
