**Yes, a gift can wait. Asking first doesn't spoil it, and receiving it doesn't create a deadline to use it.** Marcus was about to learn this beside a refrigerator that made a clicking noise whenever someone said something important.

At seven fifteen on a Wednesday in early December, Lena found him studying the paper held to her fridge by a Durham Bulls magnet.

"That's the grocery list," she said.

"I know."

"Then why do you look like you're about to give it tech support?"

Marcus stepped back. He worked in IT support for a dental software company in Raleigh. Apparently the face traveled.

Below oat milk and dishwasher tabs, Lena had written *our list*. Under that were three things they had talked about since their anniversary: a cooking class, a different kind of personal gift, and a weekend when neither of them answered work messages.

He had brought a pen.

"I was going to check something off."

"We haven't bought dishwasher tabs."

"Not that part."

She set down her bag. It had been raining, and one sleeve of her coat was dark all the way to the elbow.

"Can I get out of this first?"

"Yeah. Obviously."

He put the pen in his pocket. Then he took it out because it was hers.

---

The first personal gift had required a spreadsheet, advice from Raf, and a conversation at the farmers market that Marcus still occasionally remembered while trying to fall asleep. It had also gone well. Lena had asked if they could keep a list instead of expecting each other to guess.

So they had.

That was the trouble with doing something sensible once. He had expected it to remove all future awkwardness.

Tonight was not an anniversary. Christmas was still weeks away. But his work queue had been full of people discovering urgent problems they had ignored since September, and suddenly every unfinished thing felt late.

*If I leave it another week, she'll think I forgot.*

Lena came back in dry socks and the old sweatshirt she wore when she wanted dinner to require no further decisions.

"Okay," she said. "What's the face about?"

"I wanted to get the second thing. For Christmas. But I wanted to ask which one you meant."

"That's a good question."

He had been preparing for a harder answer.

"You don't think that ruins it?"

"You could surprise me with the wrapping."

"I'm bad at wrapping."

"I remember."

Last year's scarf had arrived in a bag with the receipt stapled to the outside. Lena still wore it. She had worn it on their anniversary, which meant he could no longer defend himself by calling the bag forgettable.

---

They heated soup and sat at the small kitchen table. Marcus opened the shared note on his phone. He had copied the fridge list into it because paper, in his experience, lacked a search function.

Lena pulled her chair closer, then stopped him before he opened a second tab.

"One thing first. I like the idea. I don't want Christmas to become the night we have to try something."

"I wasn't saying that."

"I know. I'm saying it now so neither of us has to wonder later."

The refrigerator clicked.

Marcus looked toward it.

"Even that has notes."

She smiled, but he could tell she was waiting for an actual answer.

"Okay," he said. "A present. Not a plan for the evening."

"Exactly."

Lena read the description instead of letting him summarize it. He kept his phone still, which took more effort than it should have.

"This is the kind I meant," she said.

He started to say something about ordering, then remembered there was another person in the conversation.

"For Christmas?"

"For Christmas. And we can leave it in the box until we feel like opening it."

"Even if that's January?"

"Especially if we're tired."

He shut the extra tabs.

---

Later, while Lena rinsed the bowls, he took the list off the refrigerator.

"What are you doing?"

"Making one correction."

He wrote *ask which one* beside the second item. Then he put a check next to those words, not next to the whole line.

Lena dried her hands and read it.

"Very restrained use of a checkbox."

"Professional development."

She leaned against him for a moment. He put an arm around her without deciding that the moment needed to lead anywhere. Outside, a car passed through the puddle by the curb. Inside, the soup pot still needed washing.

"You can stay," she said. "But I'm falling asleep during whatever we watch."

"That's fine."

"And don't put on something where you'll ask if I caught that."

He chose a baking show they had already seen.

---

Marcus did order the gift. He also bought wrapping paper before the store closed, which felt like planning ahead until he got home and discovered he owned no tape.

On Saturday, he brought the unopened present to Lena's place and put it in the closet where they were keeping Christmas gifts. No demonstration. No question about whether she wanted an early present. He helped carry groceries upstairs and remembered the dishwasher tabs.

Before he left, Lena checked the shared note.

"The cooking class is still on here."

"Yeah."

"Want to look at dates after the holidays?"

"After the holidays," he said.

At the door, she reached into his coat pocket and handed him the fridge pen.

"You took it anyway."

He looked down at it.

"Apparently that's my gift."

She let him keep it.
