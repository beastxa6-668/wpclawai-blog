*A short story from The Velvet Notes, the SinDept blog. The characters and events are fictional.*

The divorce papers came in a FedEx envelope on a Thursday, and I signed them at the kitchen island with the pen from my dentist's office. Twenty-six years, and the pen said *Smile! Dr. Hallorann, DDS*.

I'm fifty-two. I live in a two-bedroom townhouse in Overland Park now, the kind with a shared wall, a shared driveway, and a neighbor named Bev who knows when I get home because her dog announces it. I bought new towels. I painted the bathroom a color called Sea Salt. I learned how to reset the garbage disposal with an Allen key, which nobody had ever needed me to know before.

What I didn't do, for almost a year, was think about myself in any other way. Not like that.

My sister Carol brought it up, because Carol brings up everything. We were at Panera, splitting a bread bowl.

"Have you been, you know," she said, and wiggled her eyebrows.

"Dating? No."

"I didn't say dating."

I put my spoon down. "Carol."

"I'm just saying. You're allowed."

*Allowed* is a strange word to hear about your own body at fifty-two. I thought about it the whole drive home, down 95th Street, past the Hy-Vee and the car wash with the inflatable tube man.

*What if I've just forgotten how? What if there's nothing left to remember?*

---

The honest truth is I'd never bought anything like that. Not once. Greg and I had been married since we were twenty-six, and the closest our nightstand got to adventurous was a lavender pillow spray.

So I did what I do with everything new: I researched it like I was buying a dishwasher.

I had three requirements, and I wrote them on the back of a Hy-Vee receipt.

1. Quiet. Bev's dog hears everything. I assume Bev does too.
2. Easy to clean. I'm not going to be fussy about it at midnight.
3. Not ridiculous-looking. I don't know why this mattered. It did.

I found the [Peristaltic G-Spot Wand](https://sindept.com/products/peristaltic-g-spot-wand-10-modes/) on SinDept after an embarrassing amount of scrolling. Slender, curved. Silicone. Ten modes. Rechargeable. The description said *quiet motor*, and it said *waterproof design for easier cleaning*, and I sat back in my chair and said "huh" out loud to an empty kitchen.

I didn't know what "peristaltic" meant. I had to look it up. Wave-like movement. Rhythmic. That sounded either wonderful or like a seasickness situation, and I honestly couldn't tell which.

It was $29.99. I'd spent more on the Sea Salt paint.

The box came in plain outer packaging, nothing on the label. Bev's dog barked at the delivery driver. Bev waved at me from her porch. I waved back with the box under my arm like it was a casserole dish.

---

I charged it first, because the page said to, and because I needed something to do with my hands for an hour.

The first time I turned it on, I almost sent it back.

It moved. Not just a buzz. It *moved*, in this slow rolling ripple along the ridges, and I was holding it at arm's length like it was a fish I'd caught. I said "Oh no" to it. I'm not proud of that.

I turned it off and put it in the nightstand and went downstairs and ate a yogurt standing up.

*You're fifty-two, Ellen. You raised two kids. You can handle a silicone wand.*

---

The second night I went slower. Lowest setting. Lamp off, just the streetlight through the blinds. I'd taken a bath first, mostly to stall, partly because the bathroom was the only room in the house that felt completely mine.

The first ten minutes were nothing. Pleasant, maybe. Mostly I was listening for Bev's dog. Then I realized the only sound in the room was me breathing, and the wand was so quiet I'd almost forgotten it was on.

I changed the angle. Changed it again. I felt foolish, then less foolish, then not foolish at all.

It's hard to describe what came after without sounding like a greeting card, so I'll just say it was warm, and slow, and that the rolling feeling I'd been so suspicious of turned out to be the whole point. At some point my hand was gripping the sheet. At some point I stopped thinking about whether I was doing it right. And at the end I laughed, out loud, in my dark bedroom, and then I cried a little, which surprised me more than anything else.

Not sad crying. More like when you find something in a coat pocket from a winter you'd forgotten about.

---

In the morning I rinsed it in the bathroom sink with warm water and a little unscented soap, the way the page said, and laid it on a towel to dry. It looked very small and very innocent sitting there next to my moisturizer.

Bev's dog barked when I went to get the paper. I barked back. Bev looked up from her coffee.

"You're in a good mood," she said.

"I slept well," I said, which was true.

Carol called that afternoon to ask about Thanksgiving. Near the end she said, "So did you think about what I said?"

"About the bread bowl?"

"Ellen."

"I thought about it," I said.

She waited.

"I'm allowed," I said. "Apparently."

She laughed so loud I had to hold the phone away from my ear.
