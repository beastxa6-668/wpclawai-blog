*A short story from The Velvet Notes, the SinDept blog. The characters and events are fictional.*

The thing about being forty-one and buying your first one of these is that you are absolutely certain the entire internet is watching you.

I'm a civil engineer. I spend my days in Tucson thinking about storm drainage, which is more exciting than it sounds during monsoon season and less exciting the other eleven months. I've been divorced for three years. I have a dog named Biscuit, a pickup with 190,000 miles on it, and a group chat with my college buddies that's mostly fantasy football and pictures of brisket.

I'd been thinking about it for about a year. I'd open a tab, look for maybe four seconds, and close it like I'd touched a hot pan.

*Grown men don't need this. Grown men just—manage.*

That was the voice. It sounded a lot like my dad.

---

What changed it was dumb. My buddy Kev posted in the group chat, completely out of nowhere, a screenshot of an article about men's health. Something about how guys my age don't talk about anything. Below it he wrote, "this is us lol."

Nobody replied for an hour. Then Danny wrote, "I bought a thing last year. Best forty bucks I ever spent. Not elaborating."

Then six more guys chimed in with variations of *same*, *lol*, and one guy just posted a shrug emoji. And I sat there in my kitchen with Biscuit's head on my knee and realized I'd been carrying around this idea that I was the only one, when it turned out I was just the only one who hadn't said anything.

I didn't say anything either. I'm not that brave. But I opened a tab, and that time I didn't close it.

---

I'm an engineer, so I did it like an engineer: requirements first, product second.

1. Easy to clean. Non-negotiable. I'm not doing anything I can't fully take apart and rinse.
2. Simple controls. I have a microwave with nineteen buttons and I use one.
3. Not a giant thing that looks like it belongs in a lab.

I ended up with the [Automatic Male Masturbator with 7 Vibration & 3 Suction Modes](https://sindept.com/products/automatic-male-masturbator-7-vibration-3-suction-modes/) from SinDept. The listing said it was compact and fit comfortably in the hand. One-button control. Seven vibration modes, three suction modes. Soft textured TPE sleeve. And the line I cared about most: fully detachable for cleaning, with the sleeve coming out to wash after every use.

I read up on TPE because that's who I am. It's soft, but it's porous, which means you actually have to wash it properly and let it dry all the way. The care instructions said the same thing: mild soap or toy cleaner, rinse, dry completely before you put it back together. Water-based lube only. That honesty made me feel better, not worse.

$32.99. I've spent more on a single brisket.

---

It came in plain outer packaging, nothing on the label, which I knew because I'd read the shipping line about four times before ordering.

What I hadn't planned for was my ex-wife.

We're on fine terms. She still has a key, because she picks up Biscuit when I travel for work. And that Tuesday she let herself in to drop off his heartworm meds while I was at a site visit, and she put the package on the kitchen counter, very neatly, next to my mail.

She texted: "Left Biscuit's meds. Also you got a package. 📦"

I stood in a dry culvert in ninety-degree heat staring at that box emoji for a full minute.

*She doesn't know. It's a plain box. She doesn't know.*

I typed "Thanks!" and then deleted the exclamation point because it seemed suspicious, then put it back because no exclamation point seemed more suspicious. I'm forty-one years old.

---

I didn't touch it for a week. It sat in the hall closet next to the vacuum.

When I finally did, it was a Saturday night, and I'd charged it first like it said, and Biscuit was asleep on his bed in the living room with the door shut, and I still felt like I was doing something I'd get caught for.

The first few minutes were mostly awkward. I couldn't figure out which press changed vibration and which changed suction, and I had to pull up the product page on my phone, and the whole time I'm thinking, *this is the least romantic thing that's ever happened in this bedroom, including the divorce.*

Then I stopped thinking about it.

I won't go into all of it. I'll just say it was a lot more than I'd expected, and also a lot less dramatic, if that makes sense. Just warm, and building, and my breathing got loud enough that I worried about the dog for a second and then completely stopped worrying about the dog. At the end I laughed out loud at the ceiling, at the whole year I'd spent opening and closing tabs.

Then I took it apart in the bathroom sink, washed the sleeve with mild soap, and laid everything on a towel to dry like I was cleaning a carburetor.

---

The group chat is still mostly fantasy football. Nobody's brought it up again. But last week Kev posted another article, something about loneliness in middle-aged men, and I replied first this time.

"Real," I wrote.

That's all. Small steps. Biscuit approves.
