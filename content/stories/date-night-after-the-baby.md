At six forty, Priya put her earrings on beside a bag of frozen peas. The kitchen was the only room with a mirror she could reach without waking Anika, and the peas were there because Dev had been looking for the emergency contact list in the freezer.

"I was putting away groceries when you told me where it was," he said.

"So you checked next to the waffles."

"I followed the evidence."

The list was on the refrigerator. He had put it there himself.

They had been married eight years. Their daughter was one. Tonight was their first proper evening out with a sitter, a phrase Priya had started using as if a restaurant reservation qualified as an expedition.

The sitter, Rachel, was due in twenty minutes. Priya had written down the bedtime routine, the backup bedtime routine and the location of the book Anika preferred when she rejected both routines. Dev had printed it.

Of course he had. He had printed an itinerary when he proposed.

"Do you think this looks unfriendly?" he asked, holding the pages.

"Only the table of contents."

He removed the first sheet.

The first evening had been interrupted by Anika's fever. The next by exhaustion. Eventually there had been an evening that belonged to them.

That had not magically made Thursday nights simple.

Now Priya stood by the sink, wearing earrings she had not worn since a friend's wedding, worrying that Rachel would think the dishwasher smelled strange.

*We can talk about a private thing on the kitchen floor, but I can't leave someone in charge of the pajamas.*

The doorbell rang.

Rachel came in carrying a tote and asked where she should put her shoes. She listened while they explained Anika's routine. Then she read the pages and said, "Great. What time would you like an update?"

Priya had prepared answers about allergies, snacks and the thermostat. She had not prepared for someone making this part easy.

"After she's asleep?" she said.

"Sure."

Dev put his hand on the keys, then took it off again.

"We should go," he said.

Nobody moved.

From the living room, Anika held out a board book to Rachel.

It seemed rude to interrupt the meeting.

---

In the car, Priya checked that the phone volume was on. Dev checked that the volume was on. They compared their findings at the first red light.

The restaurant was twelve minutes away. They had chosen it because it was close, because there was parking and because neither of them had to pretend to know what to order.

The host asked if they were celebrating anything.

"Thursday," Dev said.

They sat at a small table beside a wall with a framed picture of a lemon. Priya moved her bag off the other chair. She was used to having things on every spare surface: wipes, a cup, a sock belonging to someone who refused socks.

The server brought water. They ordered. For a minute they watched another table receive bread.

"We could talk about something other than Anika," Dev said.

"Absolutely."

"How was work?"

Priya gave him the short answer she gave relatives. Fine. Busy. Same people, same meetings.

He nodded, and she heard how easily the evening could become the sort of update they exchanged while passing each other with laundry.

"Actually," she said, "there's a woman at work who keeps saying 'circle back' when she means she has lost my email."

Dev looked up.

"How many circles?"

"We are approaching a roundabout."

He laughed. Not the supportive little laugh he gave when she needed him to agree that a day had been difficult. The real one, shoulders moving.

She told him the rest.

---

The food arrived. Priya picked up her phone when the screen lit, then saw it was a shipping notification for replacement vacuum filters.

"Urgent romance supplies," she said.

Dev's phone stayed faceup on the table. He had barely touched his meal.

"Are you okay?"

"Yes."

She waited.

"I keep thinking we forgot something."

"We probably did."

"That's reassuring."

"Rachel has our numbers. The house is twelve minutes away. And she found the pajamas without an appendix."

He smiled, then looked down at his hands.

"I thought you were the nervous one."

"I am. We can both be nervous."

That appeared to help more than telling him there was nothing to worry about.

Her phone lit again. Rachel had sent a short message: Anika was asleep. The picture showed the closed bedroom door, not a sleeping child they could zoom in on and inspect for evidence of discomfort.

Priya showed Dev. He read it twice.

"Dessert?" she asked.

"We said we'd be back by nine."

"It's seven fifty."

He checked his watch, as if she might be using a more optimistic time zone.

They ordered one dessert and two forks.

---

They did talk about Anika. Of course they did. They talked about the way she handed people books backward, and about the particular offended noise she made when someone peeled a banana incorrectly.

Then Dev told Priya something he had been considering at work. She asked a question instead of offering the quickest solution. He took a while to answer. There was time for that.

When they left, Priya realized she had not checked her phone during dessert.

At home, Rachel gave them the kind of report that made an evening sound wonderfully uneventful. Book, pajamas, a brief objection, sleep. Dev paid her. Priya thanked her without apologizing for anything.

The kitchen was still their kitchen. There was a spoon under the high chair. The printed instructions sat on the counter with a mug on top.

Dev picked up the pages.

"Useful?" he asked.

"Very. The mug needed a coaster."

He left them where they were.

Before taking off her earrings, Priya opened the calendar.

"Same time next month?"

"Do we need a different restaurant?"

"I liked the lemon."

He came over and looked at the date she had chosen. Then he took his phone out and checked it properly, instead of saying probably and leaving her to arrange the rest.

"That works," he said. "I'll ask Rachel."

Priya shut the calendar. She put her earrings in the little dish by the door, where she would see them again.
