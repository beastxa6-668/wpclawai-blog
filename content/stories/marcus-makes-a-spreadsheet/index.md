*A short story from The Velvet Notes, the SinDept blog. The characters and events are fictional.*

Marcus had a spreadsheet for everything. Grocery prices at three different stores. His 401(k). The exact number of minutes it took to drive from his apartment in Raleigh to his girlfriend's place in Durham depending on whether I-40 was doing its thing.

He did not have a spreadsheet for this, and it was killing him.

He was twenty-nine. He worked in IT support for a dental software company, which meant he spent eight hours a day telling receptionists to restart things. He and Lena had been together a year and two months. Their anniversary was in three weeks. Last year he'd gotten her a nice scarf and a dinner reservation, and she'd been sweet about it, and he had lain awake later knowing it was a scarf.

This year, a month ago, after a late night and most of a bottle of wine, Lena had said something.

"I wish we tried more stuff." She'd said it into his shoulder, half asleep. "Not, like, weird stuff. Just—stuff."

Then she'd fallen asleep, and in the morning she hadn't mentioned it, and he hadn't either.

*She meant me. She meant I'm boring. She meant the scarf.*

---

He told his friend Raf about it, carefully, at a brewery on a Thursday, staring at a pint like it had the answer written on the bottom.

"So get her something," Raf said.

"Like what?"

Raf shrugged. "Something for both of you. Not just for her. Then it's not like you're saying, 'Here, fix yourself.' It's like, 'Hey, let's do this together.'"

Marcus sat with that the whole drive home. It was the first useful thing anyone had said.

---

That weekend he did what he did with everything: research. He opened eleven tabs. He closed nine of them because the pictures made him feel like he was going to get fired just by looking at them on his own laptop in his own apartment. He had a rule: if he wouldn't want Lena's mom to see the product page over his shoulder, it was out.

He found the [Vibrating Dual Cock Ring with 9 Modes](https://sindept.com/products/vibrating-dual-cock-ring-9-modes-longer-stronger-more/) on SinDept late Sunday night. The photos were just the product on a dark background. Nothing that would make him close the laptop.

He actually made the spreadsheet then. Four columns: *What it says. Does that matter. Questions. Price.*

- 9 vibration modes. *Matters. Neither of us knows what we like yet.*
- Dual ring design, soft and stretchy. *Okay.*
- Premium silicone + ABS. *Looked this up. Silicone = good.*
- One-touch power button. *Matters a lot. I will panic if there's an app.*
- About 3.54 × 2.2 inches. *Small. Not scary.*
- Shared vibration for both partners. *This is the whole point.*
- $14.99. *Cheaper than the scarf.*

That last line stopped him. The scarf had been sixty dollars, and it was sitting in Lena's closet.

He ordered it at 12:41 a.m., then lay in bed and stared at the ceiling until 1:30, convinced he'd made a terrible mistake.

---

It came in plain outer packaging, nothing on the label. Of course it came on the one day his building's front-desk guy was handing packages out personally.

"Something good?"

"Cables," Marcus said. "For work."

"Nice." The guy went back to his phone.

Marcus took the elevator holding the box with both hands like it might go off.

---

He told her first. That was the thing he'd decided. Not a surprise. Not something that just shows up in the bedroom. A conversation, in daylight, with clothes on.

He did it at the farmer's market in Durham, which in hindsight was not the best location, while Lena was holding a bag of peaches.

"So you know how you said you wanted to try more stuff."

She stopped walking. "When did I say that?"

"In August. After the wine."

She went pink, right up to her ears. "Oh my God. You remembered that?"

"I think about it a lot," he said, and then realized how that sounded and said "Not in a weird way," and she laughed so hard she had to put the peaches down on somebody's table.

"Okay," she said, when she could breathe. "What'd you do?"

He told her. She listened, and then she asked a question he hadn't put in the spreadsheet.

"Did you get it for you or for me?"

"For us," he said. "That's—Raf said—" He gave up. "For us."

She looked at him for a long time. Then she kissed him, right there by the peaches, in front of a guy selling microgreens.

---

The anniversary dinner was at the same restaurant as last year. Lena wore the scarf, which he hadn't expected and which made something in his chest go tight.

Later, at her place, nobody was very smooth. He had the instructions on his phone. He'd read them maybe ten times. He still had to stop halfway, turn the lamp back on, and read them again.

"Are you checking the manual?" Lena said, propped on one elbow.

"I'm being thorough."

"You're being Marcus."

The first mode was barely anything. He said, "Is that it?" and she said, "Click it again," and he did, and then they were both laughing, and then she pulled him closer by the back of the neck and they weren't.

He didn't think about his spreadsheet. He didn't think about the scarf. He thought about her breath against his jaw, and how warm the room got, and the way she said his name like a question she already knew the answer to.

Afterward she lay with her head on his chest, tracing circles on his arm.

"We should make a list," she said. "Of other stuff."

He was quiet for a second.

"I already have a spreadsheet," he said.

She laughed so hard the bed shook.
