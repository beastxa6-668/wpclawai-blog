**The best sex toy gift for your wife, husband or partner is one they have said they would welcome.** Start with their preferences, then compare controls and care routines. Black Friday and Cyber Monday are good occasions to make a thoughtful shortlist—not a reason to turn an intimate gift into a surprise test. If you are unsure, make choosing together the gift.

Ask a specific, low-pressure question: “Would you like us to pick something together, or would you rather I stayed with a different kind of gift?” Leave room for a genuine no. A gift should not imply that your partner needs fixing or owes you a shared experience.

## What makes a useful gift for a husband or male partner?

The same conversation comes first. Ask whether they prefer a simple manual design, powered controls, or no intimate product at all. A product category is not a guess about someone's masculinity, confidence or performance.

## How can I keep the delivery private?

Before ordering, check the current delivery policy and make sure your partner is comfortable with the delivery address and who might receive the parcel. Packaging privacy is different from privacy on shared email, shared devices or payment notifications.

Our [discreet shipping explainer](/gift-guides/what-discreet-shipping-really-means/) covers the questions to ask. Read the current checkout information before relying on an arrival date. If a gift is time-sensitive, separate processing time from transit time and allow for weekends and holiday disruption. Do not promise a date to the recipient on the strength of a general estimate.

## What should I check before wrapping it?

Leave intimate products sealed for the recipient. Read the current return policy before ordering; do not assume an opened item can be exchanged just because the preference was wrong. Let your partner be the first person to inspect and decide whether to use it.

Keep the instructions and charging accessories together. Include a note with permission to change their mind: “I thought you might like this, but we can choose something else.” A quiet conversation is usually more useful than a joke in front of friends or relatives.

For more help starting that conversation, see [how to bring up trying something new](/relationships/how-to-bring-up-trying-something-new/). The point of the gift is being attentive, not making a holiday deadline feel more important than comfort.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an intimate gift be a surprise?

Only within preferences your partner has already shared. If the category itself would be a surprise, ask first or choose together.

### Should I choose by gender labels?

Choose by the recipient's stated preferences and the product's intended use. “For him” and “for her” are browsing labels, not enough information to make the decision.

### What if we are not ready to buy?

A shared shortlist can wait. There is no need to order something simply because a shopping holiday is on the calendar.

### Is a more complicated product a more thoughtful gift?

Not necessarily. A design that is easy for the recipient to control, clean and store may be a better match than one with more functions.
