Maya got home from a twelve-hour shift, dropped her keys in the bowl by the door and remembered, halfway through taking off her shoes, that [the little cat-shaped suction toy](https://sindept.com/products/cat-shaped-clitoral-suction-toy-7-modes/?utm_source=velvetnotes&utm_medium=blog&utm_campaign=how-to-clean-and-store-your-toys&utm_content=inline) was still on the nightstand where she'd left it two nights ago. Not in a drawer. Not in anything. Just sitting there next to a hair tie and a half-finished glass of water.

She works in an emergency department. She can tell you exactly how long to scrub before touching a patient. Her own nightstand had apparently been running on vibes.

So she stood at the bathroom sink at 8:40 p.m., still in her scrub top, and read the care instructions properly for the first time.

## Quick answer

> Wash before and after every use with warm water and mild, unscented soap or a toy cleaner made for the material. Rinse, pat dry, then let it air-dry completely. Charge it only when it's dry, and don't use it while it's plugged in. Store it on its own, in a clean pouch or box, somewhere cool and dry.

## What do I actually need?

Less than you think:

- **Mild, unscented soap.** Fragrance and harsh detergents can irritate skin, and you're rinsing this off something that touches very sensitive skin.
- **Warm water.** Not boiling, unless the instructions for that exact item say it can take it.
- **A lint-free cloth or clean towel.** Paper towels leave fluff on silicone.
- **A pouch or a small box.** A cotton drawstring bag, a clean zip pouch or the original box all work.

A toy cleaner is optional. If you buy one, check that it's meant for the material you have.

## How do I clean it, step by step?

1. **Check it's switched off and unplugged.** Close any charging cover.
2. **Check the water rating.** Fully waterproof toys (often listed as IPX7) can be rinsed under the tap. Splash-resistant ones can't go under running water; wipe them with a damp soapy cloth instead.
3. **Wash with warm water and a little soap.** Work it into seams, ridges, buttons and any opening. On an air-pulse toy, the soft opening and the area around it deserve the most attention.
4. **Rinse until no soap is left.** Soap residue is a common cause of a slightly sticky feel.
5. **Pat dry, then air-dry.** Leave it on a clean towel until it's completely dry, including any openings and around the buttons.

Maya's cat-shaped toy is a silicone air-pulse toy rated IPX7, so the tap was fine. Her instructions also said to clean it before and after every use, and that it shouldn't be used while charging. The second line was news to her.

## Charging: the step people skip

- **Dry first.** Moisture and charging ports are not friends, even on waterproof toys.
- **Don't use it while it's charging.** Many rechargeable toys say this in their care instructions; follow it.
- **Use the cable it came with.** Magnetic and USB connectors aren't interchangeable, and a loose fit can heat up or fail to charge.
- **Don't leave it to charge unattended for days.** Unplug once it's full.

## How should I store it?

| Situation | What works | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| One toy | A cotton or satin pouch in a drawer | Loose in a drawer with coins, keys and lint |
| Several toys | Separate pouches, or a box with dividers | Different materials pressed together |
| Shared home | A box that closes, kept in your own space | A shared bathroom cabinet |
| Travel | Its own pouch, charged, in a toiletry bag | A suitcase pocket with no cover |

Storing toys separately matters because some materials react with each other over time, especially softer, realistic-feel materials. A pouch also keeps lint and dust off, which saves you a rewash.

Keep the storage spot cool and dry. A windowsill or the top of a radiator is not cool and dry.

## Signs it's time to replace it

- The surface stays sticky or tacky after a proper rinse.
- There are cracks, tears or a damaged seam.
- It smells off even after washing and fully drying.
- The charging port is corroded or the toy no longer holds a charge.

Don't try to rescue a damaged surface with a stronger cleaner, alcohol or bleach. If it's damaged, stop using it.

## Lubricant and cleanup

Water-based lubricant rinses off easily and is the usual pairing for silicone toys. Silicone-based lubricant can damage some silicone surfaces over time. ## Maya's version of a routine

It took her about three minutes. Wash, rinse, towel, leave it on the edge of the sink while she brushed her teeth, then into a soft pouch from an old pair of earbuds, then into the drawer instead of onto the nightstand.

The next morning she noticed the drawer had become, without any announcement, a slightly more organized drawer. She didn't make a big deal about it. She did buy a second pouch.

## FAQ

### Can I put a toy in the dishwasher?

Only if the instructions for that exact item say so. Most rechargeable toys have motors, batteries and seals that a dishwasher's heat and detergent can damage.

### How often should I clean it?

Before and after every use. If it has been in storage for a while, a quick rinse before use is still a good idea.

### Is hand soap okay?

A mild, unscented soap is fine for most silicone toys. Skip anything heavily perfumed, antibacterial with harsh additives, or oily.

### What if a toy is shared between partners?

Clean it between people, and consider a condom over insertable toys when sharing or switching between body areas. If you notice irritation, pain or unusual discharge, stop using the toy and talk to a doctor.

### Do waterproof toys need special drying?

Yes. Waterproof means water won't get inside; it doesn't mean the outside dries itself. Let it air-dry fully before charging or storing.
