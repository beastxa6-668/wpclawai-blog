Fourteen months of this. They were good at it, mostly. They knew each other's weekly schedules by heart. They had a shared playlist and a running joke about the Cubs. What they didn't have was a way to make a Tuesday night feel like anything other than two people looking at small rectangles.

## Why does distance make intimacy feel harder?

Because most of the easy stuff disappears. No casual touch on the way past the kitchen, no falling asleep together, no reading each other's body language from across the room. Long-distance couples often end up with plenty of conversation and very little closeness.

The fix usually isn't more calls. It's making some of those calls about something other than logistics.

## How do we schedule around different time zones?

- **Pick a standing night.** One evening a week that's protected, not "whenever we're both free."
- **Use the later time zone as the anchor.** If one of you starts work at 7 a.m., a 10 p.m. call for the other person may be 11 p.m. for them.
- **Agree on a soft cutoff.** Knowing a call ends by a certain time makes it easier to relax into it.
- **Have a rain check rule.** If one person is exhausted, rescheduling is fine and it doesn't mean anything.

Jordan and Sam were only an hour apart, which helped. Couples on opposite coasts or in different countries have to be more deliberate, but the same rules apply.

## What should we talk about before trying anything new?

Have this conversation when nobody's already undressed. A walk on the phone or a quiet call works.

1. **What you're curious about.** Be specific: "I'd like to try a video date that's more than talking" is easier to answer than "Want to try something?"
2. **What's off the table.** Each person gets a no list, and nobody has to justify it.
3. **What "stop" sounds like.** On a call, a clear word or phrase works better than hoping the other person notices a face on a small screen.
4. **Privacy.** Agree that nobody records or screenshots without asking first, every time.
5. **Check-ins afterward.** A text the next morning, "How did that feel for you?", keeps it from becoming awkward later.

If one of you feels pressured, slow down. Curiosity should go both ways. Our guide on [how to bring up trying something new](/relationships/how-to-bring-up-trying-something-new/) has opening lines that work over the phone, too.

## How do we make video calls feel less awkward?

| Problem | Small fix |
|---|---|
| Worried about roommates or neighbors | Headphones with a mic; a fan or music in the room |
| Phone dies mid-call | Charge it first, or keep it plugged in on a stand |
| Bad angle, constant readjusting | A cheap phone stand or a stack of books |
| Notifications popping up | Do Not Disturb on both phones |
| Laggy connection | Wi-Fi instead of mobile data; close other apps |
| Feeling self-conscious | Lower lighting; camera off at first is allowed |

It's also fine if the first few attempts are mostly laughing. That counts.

## Where do app-controlled toys fit in?

Some toys connect to a phone app, so a partner can change the pattern or intensity from their own phone. For long-distance couples, the appeal is simple: one person's choices have a physical effect on the other, which a video call alone can't do.

Before you rely on one:

- **Read how it connects.** Some apps only work nearby over Bluetooth; others can be controlled from farther away through the app. Product descriptions usually say which.
- **Look at what the app asks for.** Check the permissions it wants on your phone and whether you need to make an account.
- **Do a test run alone first.** Learn the settings yourself before handing over control.
- **Agree on who controls what.** Handing over control is a trust thing; talk about it like one.
- **Charge it before the call.** Then clean it before and after use, following its care instructions.

## Small things that close the gap

- Send something physical: a handwritten note, a shirt that smells like you, the same snack to eat on a call.
- Watch the same movie, started at the same second.
- Keep a shared doc of things you want to do on the next visit.
- Count down to the next visit together, out loud.

## FAQ

### How often should long-distance couples have intimate calls?

There's no right number. What matters is that both people want the frequency you land on, and that skipping one isn't treated as rejection.

### Is it normal to feel awkward on video?

Very. Lighting, angles and lag make it weird for almost everyone at first. It usually gets easier with a few tries and a sense of humor.

### Can app-controlled toys be used from another city?

Some can. It depends on how the app connects, so check the product description and do a test with your partner before a planned night.

### What if one of us isn't comfortable with any of this?

Then that's the answer for now. Closeness at a distance can also be a long call, a shared show or a letter. Revisit the conversation later if both of you want to.
